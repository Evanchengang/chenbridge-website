Export Packaging

Common Export Packaging Challenges

ChenBridge Insights · 6 min read

Most export packaging failures are not bad luck — they are predictable problems that were not addressed before production. Here are the issues we see most often, and how to prevent them.

1. Moisture and Humidity Damage

Ocean freight exposes packaging to weeks of humidity and temperature swings. Corrugated board absorbs moisture and loses strength; wood can develop mold; metal parts corrode.

  • Specify moisture-resistant liners or coatings for sea freight
  • Use desiccants and vapor barriers inside packaging for sensitive goods
  • Consider container condition — a damp container ruins good packaging

2. Collapsed Cartons and Stacking Failures

Cartons stacked in containers and warehouses carry significant loads for weeks. Box compression strength that looks fine on day one can fail on day thirty — especially after absorbing humidity. Match board grade and flute type to real stacking loads, not just product weight, and test the weakest configuration: a full pallet, bottom carton.

3. Non-Compliant Wood Packaging

Wood packaging moving internationally must meet ISPM-15: heat treatment and an official mark. Non-compliant pallets and crates can be rejected, re-packaged, or destroyed at the destination port — at your cost. Confirm your supplier has legitimate ISPM-15 treatment capability and ask to see the mark before shipment.

4. Unclear Specifications

"Strong box" is not a specification. Neither is "good quality wood." Vague requirements get interpreted by the factory — usually toward lower cost. Write specifications in measurable terms: board grade, ECT or burst strength, timber thickness, treatment standard, print colors in Pantone, tolerances in millimeters.

5. Missing Pre-Shipment Confirmation

Even a good factory has bad days. Without a final check before goods leave the factory, problems travel 10,000 kilometers before anyone notices. Photos of finished goods, packing lists, and — for larger orders — a pre-shipment inspection close the gap between "produced" and "approved."

The Bottom Line

Export packaging problems are cheaper to prevent than to fix. Confirm specifications in writing, verify compliance requirements early, and check goods before they ship. Our Export Packaging Requirements Checklist covers the key points — or contact us to discuss your shipment.
